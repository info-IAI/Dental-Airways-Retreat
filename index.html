Airway Dentistry Retreat at Miraval

A Two‑Day Immersive Experience for Dentists Ready to Bring Airway Awareness Into Everyday Practice

We are two general dentists who began working together in 2020, each with a foundational knowledge of airway dentistry. Over the past several years, we've developed practical ways to incorporate that knowledge into the realities of a busy general practice. We've found that dentists are often in a unique position to identify airway-related issues that have gone unrecognized, providing patients with answers to longstanding concerns and a clear path forward. Our patients often express deep appreciation for finally having these issues identified.

As our understanding grew, so did our collaborative network. Today, we work closely with ENTs, PTs, and myofunctional therapists. These professionals are essential in managing multifactorial airway issues. At this retreat, two ENTs will join us to share their perspective and demonstrate how dentists can meaningfully collaborate with medical colleagues to provide more comprehensive care.

Your faculty

We are two general dentists who began working together in 2020. Each of us had foundational knowledge of airway dentistry, and over the past seven years we've discovered practical, realistic ways to incorporate that knowledge into a busy general practice.

What we've learned is simple but powerful: dentists are often one of the first healthcare providers to identify airway-related concerns. Patients frequently tell us they're grateful someone finally connected the dots.

Our journey has led us to build a strong collaborative network of ENTs, PTs, and myofunctional therapists. All are essential partners in managing multifactorial airway issues. Two ENTs (general and pediatric) will join us at the retreat to share their perspective and demonstrate how dentists can collaborate meaningfully with medical colleagues.

Dr. Meira Berman, DMD

Co-founder

Meira holds her DMD from the University of Pennsylvania and has pursued advanced training through the Kois Center, the Clinical Mastery Series, and the University of Minnesota's postgraduate continuum. Her commitment to learning led her to airway, where she discovered its relevance across every aspect of patient care. She co-founded IAI (Integrated Airway Institute) around a simple conviction that the right people, the right setting, and focused time together can fundamentally shift how a dentist sees every patient who walks through the door.

Dr. Shira Levy, DMD

Co-founder

Shira sees patients at Elements Dental Studio in Evanston, Illinois, where airway, sleep, TMJ, and early orthodontic intervention define her practice. She came to this work through years of CE and a growing certainty that dentists are missing patients who need them. Her goal for attendees is simple: to leave with the ability to look at a patient they've seen a hundred times and recognize the airway in a whole new way.

Dr. Colleen Plein, MD

ENT

Colleen is a board-certified otolaryngologist (ENT) specializing in sinus and sleep-related breathing disorders, with more than a decade of experience helping patients breathe easier, sleep better, and reduce chronic pain related to airway dysfunction. She trained at the University of Chicago and SUNY Downstate. Her approach centers on collaborative, unhurried care. Rather than treating breathing and sleep issues in isolation, she works to understand their full impact on a patient's health and daily functioning. She joins the IAI with the conviction that when dentists understand the true scope of disrupted breathing, it fundamentally changes how they approach patient care.

Dr. Micah Berman, MD

Pediatric ENT

Micah leads the ENT division at Gillette Children's Specialty Healthcare in Minneapolis, where his practice centers on pediatric airway, obstructive sleep apnea, and craniofacial conditions that require coordinated care across specialties. He trained at Penn, Cornell, Columbia, and Children's Hospital of Wisconsin. He comes to IAI after a career spent receiving referrals that often arrive too late, with the conviction that earlier recognition and understanding by dentists can change the course of care.
