Course information

Structure drives function.

One organizing idea runs through both days: the mouth is the front door to the airway, and the craniofacial structure you already manage decides how a patient breathes and sleeps. Maximize the structure and function follows. Lecture format throughout, taught on six projected teaching cases, with a guest otolaryngologist for the second day.

Detailed agenda in development. Here is the shape of it.

01

The paradigm and the eye

Why breathing belongs at the center of comprehensive dentistry, how the dentofacial complex sets airway volume, and how to screen, phenotype and read the airway in three minutes at the chair.

02

Growth, structure & the window

Reading craniofacial development across age groups, identifying the growth guidance window in younger patients, and connecting structural findings to appliance and orthopedic options.

03

Sleep, the airway & the adult patient

Oral appliance therapy selection and titration, surgical options, and building the medical relationships — ENT, sleep medicine, myofunctional — that the work depends on.

04

The ENT in the room

Day two opens the medical side: nasal and upper airway anatomy, surgical decision-making, what a good referral letter looks like from the receiving end, and building the joint and the site while you advance the airway.

Learning slows down when the room does. Afternoons here are yours.
